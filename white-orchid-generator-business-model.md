# Business Model Research: White Orchid Social Media Generator as a Studio Offering

## The tool, reframed

Right now this is an internal build for one client: a brand-locked generator that turns The White Orchid's palette, type, and logo into ready-to-export social posts. The question isn't whether to sell it as standalone SaaS. It's whether it becomes a repeatable line item in the studio: a "branded content engine" you build for each client, the way you'd build a brand guideline PDF, except it keeps producing value after delivery.

That reframe matters because it changes the buyer. A SaaS product needs thousands of self-serve users to work. A white-label studio offering needs a handful of clients willing to pay for a tool built around their brand specifically, sold alongside the creative direction they already trust you for.

## Three models, and what they actually cost to run

**1. Productized build (one-time fee per client)**
You customize the generator's templates, palette, and copy logic for each new client and hand it over as a deliverable, similar to how you'd hand over a brand book. Comparable productized-service setups in design run $2,500 to $15,000 per build depending on complexity. This fits your studio's current shape best: no ongoing infrastructure to maintain, revenue lands per project, and it slots naturally into a client engagement that already includes brand direction.

**2. Build + hosted retainer (setup fee plus monthly access)**
Same customized build, but you host it and charge monthly for access, updates, and new templates as the brand evolves. Productized design retainers in this space run $1,500 to $10,000 a month; a lighter version for a tool like this (not a full design retainer) would likely sit at the low end, more like $200 to $500 a month per client, closer to what agencies charge for white-labeled AI content tools ($300 to $1,500/month) once you strip out the marketing-automation layer those tools include. This is the model that turns a one-off deliverable into recurring revenue without needing hundreds of customers.

**3. Multi-tenant SaaS (one platform, many clients self-serve)**
You rebuild the generator so any client can plug in their own brand kit and use it independently, then charge a subscription. Comparable tools: Templated.io ($29/month, 1,000 renders, includes a white-label embeddable editor built for exactly this use case), Bannerbear ($49/month), Placid ($15 for 500 credits), Predis.ai ($32/month). This is the most scalable model on paper, but it's also the most expensive to build and maintain well, and it competes directly with tools that already do this generically. It only makes sense if you want the studio to become a software business, not a creative one.

## What fits the studio right now

Given where you are (winding into a focused creative role, running House of Perena, SuperteamSG, and White Orchid as client work), model 2 is the strongest fit: a productized build with a modest hosted retainer. It lets each client project fund the tool that will differentiate the next pitch, without turning you into a software vendor. The tool becomes evidence of craft ("we don't just hand you a brand book, we hand you the machine that keeps it consistent") rather than a separate product line competing for its own customers.

Templated.io's approach is worth studying directly: they built the white-label editor as the differentiator that lets agencies resell without becoming an engineering team. If you ever move toward model 3, that's the pattern to copy rather than building a generic multi-tenant app from scratch.

## Rough numbers to anchor a pitch

A build-plus-retainer package for a client like White Orchid could look like: $3,000 to $6,000 for the initial branded build (comparable to a mid-size productized design engagement), then $250 to $500 a month for hosting, template updates, and light support. At three to five studio clients, that's $750 to $2,500 a month in recurring revenue on top of project fees, at high margin since the underlying app is already built and just needs re-skinning.
